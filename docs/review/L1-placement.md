# L1 - Trial placement of both floor plans

Layout has started. This step produces a **scripted trial placement of every part on both outlines**, checked with KiCad's own DRC, so you can choose between v1 and v2 on evidence. Nothing is routed yet.

**Files:** [`L1-placement-v1.png`](L1-placement-v1.png) (260 x 160 mm), [`L1-placement-v2.png`](L1-placement-v2.png) (220 x 140 mm), DRC reports [`L1-drc-v1.rpt`](L1-drc-v1.rpt) and [`L1-drc-v2.rpt`](L1-drc-v2.rpt), boards `hardware/lcec-io-v1.kicad_pcb` and `hardware/lcec-io-v2.kicad_pcb` (open in KiCad). Generated by `scripts/layout_build.py` from the schematic's netlist, so the boards always match the schematic.

## Method

- KiCad's own Python module builds each board: 315 footprints from the project and standard libraries, 323 nets from the schematic netlist, four copper layers, board outline on Edge.Cuts.
- Fixed positions come from the floor plans: terminals on the edges, the relay strip (socket, fuse, terminal per row), RJ45 jacks, MCU, LAN9252, DC-DC modules. The optocouplers and the MPG, relay and analog isolators are placed straddling their barrier line, with their field-side pins on the field side.
- Every other part (about 250) is placed by an automatic first pass: inside its domain region, as close as possible to the parts it connects through signal nets, without overlap.

## Result

| | v1: 260 x 160 mm | v2: 220 x 140 mm |
|---|---|---|
| Parts placed on the board | 308 of 315 | 308 of 315 |
| Estimated signal wirelength (218 nets, power and ground excluded) | 9.2 m | 8.3 m |
| DRC: courtyard overlaps, pad clearance, shorts, edge clearance, holes | **none** | **none** |
| DRC: unrouted connections | 499 (expected, nothing routed) | 499 |
| DRC: silkscreen warnings | about 400, cosmetic | about 400, cosmetic |

Two footprint problems found by DRC were fixed in the project library (`scripts/fix_symbols.py`, regenerates them): the RJ45 jack's two unnamed 3.3 mm mounting holes were drawn as plated pads and are now non-plated holes, and the three radial 100 uF capacitors had a one-line courtyard, now a proper box. The datasheet pin check is still clean for all 44 symbols.

## What the trial shows

- **Both outlines hold every part.** v2 is 10 % shorter in estimated wirelength; v1 has more free area (43 % against 58 % footprint coverage), which helps routing and the isolation distances.
- **Not placed: 7 of the 8 unfitted relay snubber capacitors** (C712 to C718, the 18 x 8 mm film footprints). The mains-side strip has no free area for them in either plan. They are unfitted, so the options are: place them on the bottom side under the relay strip, use a smaller footprint, or drop the snubber footprints. They are parked outside the board outline for now.
- **v2 is not yet realistic for its back terminal row:** both rows use the current KF2EDGR footprint, because the top-entry plug and its header (you chose top-entry) are not selected yet. Their depth may not fit the 12.5 mm row.
- **Not yet checked** (next steps): isolation clearances and creepage between the domains and on the mains side, the exact wire-entry direction of the terminals and the opening direction of the RJ45 jacks, decoupling placement at the MCU and LAN9252 pins, thermal.

## Decision for you

Which outline for routing? My view: **v1** unless board size matters most. It needs no new part and leaves room for isolation spacing; v2 saves 40 x 20 mm of board but needs the back-row part, is denser and leaves no room for the snubbers.

## Next steps after your choice

1. 4-layer stackup, net classes and the isolation and creepage rules (24 V domains, 230 VAC relay contacts, the routed slot).
2. Decoupling and clock placement by hand around the MCU and LAN9252; relay snubbers.
3. Ground and power planes, then routing (EtherCAT pairs first), DRC, 3D check.
4. Manufacturing files and a final review.
